Case study · Operations that ran on favors

Partner onboarding cut from 205 to 90 business days.

A public fintech company, 2024 to 2026. The problem: work that crosses several teams, with nobody able to see what is at risk.

The situation

Every new partner took 205 business days to bring on.

A public fintech company ran a partner program in which bringing on each new partner needed several teams, including legal, deal desk and finance, to do their part before launch. I was building the operations function behind that program from the ground up. No onboarding process, order-form workflow or partner data infrastructure had existed before.

What was broken

It ran off a checklist and one person's persistence.

Onboarding was a checklist. One person chased other teams for favors, and nobody could see what was at risk until it was late. Each team was doing its part in good faith. Nothing connected the parts to a date.

What I changed

A shared schedule, agreed team by team.

From checklist to schedule

I turned the checklist into a shared schedule and agreed a named owner for every task, one team at a time. Any delay now showed its effect on the launch date at once, so priority calls became short and factual.

Self-serve in place of forms

Manual partner order forms were replaced with self-serve dashboards and automatic notifications. Legal, deal desk and finance stopped re-keying and chasing the same information.

Visibility for leadership

The same data fed the first executive reporting on partner pipeline and program health, so leadership could see the program without asking for an update.

The result

90 business days, and conversations about facts.

Onboarding dropped from 205 to 90 business days. The order-form change freed 90 hours a month across three teams. The larger change was in how decisions got made: when a delay visibly moves a launch date, nobody has to argue about priority.

What transfers

In a regulated business, legal, compliance, product and sales all have to agree before anything ships. That is exactly where work stalls between teams. A shared plan with a named owner for each handoff works anywhere several functions must sign off on the same outcome.
