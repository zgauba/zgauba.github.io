Case study · AI bought, work unchanged

An AI agent that catches contract errors at signing, not at billing.

A public fintech company, 2024 to 2026. The problem: “We’ve invested in AI and the work hasn’t changed.”

The situation

Contract errors surfaced at billing, too late to fix with the client.

At a public fintech company, figures in a client contract, such as prices and quantities, changed during negotiation and did not always reach the system of record. The errors surfaced only at billing, too late to fix with the client.

What was broken

A manual check nobody had time to do well.

Comparing a signed contract line by line against the system is slow, dull and easy to get wrong. It is exactly the kind of step that gets skipped under deadline.

What I changed

Put the agent inside the live process.

The contract check

I put an AI agent into deal approval. It read each signed contract and compared it with the system of record, then showed every difference to the people approving the deal before final approval. Errors were caught at signing.

The same pattern, elsewhere

Requests to the team used to arrive by chat, by email and in the corridor, and nobody knew what had been solved before. Another agent read each request, checked whether the problem had already been solved or another team was already working on it, scored it, and put it in one queue. People made the priority calls.

The rule behind both

AI handles synthesis and drafting. People own judgment and decisions. An agent belongs in a process that already has clear steps, clean data and a named owner. Automation on top of a broken process only makes the mess faster.

The result

30+ agents in production, doing work people used to skip.

More than 30 AI agents went into production across analytical and reporting work. The contract agent meant every difference was visible before a deal was approved. The measure I care about is not how many agents were built. It is whether the daily work changed.

What transfers

Most companies that have invested in AI and seen little change have bought tools without touching the process. The fix is to find the manual, error-prone steps, put an agent into the live process at that point, and keep a person checking the output.
