Case study · Growth outrunning the team

Clients grew eightfold. The team grew threefold. Retention held at 99%.

Segmint, 2018 to 2022. The problem: “We’re growing, service is slipping, and we can’t hire our way out.”

The situation

A startup that couldn't hire a manager for every new batch of clients.

Segmint was a marketing and customer-data platform for banks and credit unions. When I took over client success in 2018 it served 20 clients. The client list was growing, and the usual answer, adding a client success manager for every new group of clients, was not one a startup could afford.

What was broken

Every manager was expected to do everything.

Each client success manager was project manager, analyst, trainer, strategist, renewal driver and escalation handler at once. That model burns out the best people first, because they are the ones who try to do all of it. Adding people to it only scales the dysfunction.

What I changed

Fix the model first, then hire into it.

Split the job

Senior managers kept only the client relationship, so each could look after many more clients. One shared client care team took the admin for all of them. Onboarding, operations and complex implementation became their own functions, built early, before the volume demanded them.

One question before every hire

Before opening a role I asked what it would take for a system to do this instead of a person. The answer was not always automation. Sometimes it was a cleaner process or a better onboarding template. Tools moved up in steps, from spreadsheets to a dedicated customer success platform.

Named ownership, specific standards, trusted data

  • Ownership was unambiguous. Every client, every escalation and every renewal had a named owner.
  • Standards were specific. What a healthy client looks like at 90 days, at first renewal and at 18 months, not in general.
  • Data was trusted. Health scoring and lifecycle tracking were in place before they were needed, so leadership could see the portfolio without asking for a manual update.

The result

Retention became the output of a system.

Clients grew from 20 to 165 while the team grew 230%, and monthly recurring revenue rose from $130K to $1.1MM. Retention held at 99% through Segmint's $135MM exit to Alkami Technology in 2022. Client care became the route for junior hires to grow into managers. I repeated the pattern after the exit: clients up 40% on a 23% headcount increase.

What transfers

Scaling a client-facing team is an operating model problem, not a hiring problem. Get the model right and the headcount you need becomes clear and manageable. This applies to any business with recurring client relationships, not only software.
