Three problems I'm brought in to solve:

“We're growing, service is slipping, and we can't hire our way out.”
I redesign how the team works and what tools it uses, so each person can serve more clients well. At Segmint, clients grew 725% while the team grew 230%, at 99% retention. Repeated at Alkami: clients up 40% on a 23% headcount increase.
“We've invested in AI and the work hasn't changed.”
I find the manual, error-prone steps and put AI agents into the live process. AI handles synthesis and drafting; people own judgment and decisions. 30+ agents in production.
“We bought a company and the two still don't run as one.”
I run the integration office: one plan, a named owner for each handoff, visible risks. Most recently for a $400M acquisition, one of three integrations I've worked on.

The edge on all three: I've done it in regulated financial services, where legal, compliance, product and sales all have to agree before anything ships.

Proof

What it looks like in practice.

Partner onboarding: 205 to 90 business days
Onboarding ran off a checklist, with one person chasing other teams for favors and nobody able to see what was at risk. I turned it into a shared schedule with a named owner for every task. Any delay showed its effect on the launch date at once, so priority calls became short and factual. Replacing manual order forms with self-serve dashboards freed another 90 hours a month across legal, deal desk and finance.
Segmint: 20 to 165 clients at 99% retention
A startup can't add a client success manager for every new batch of clients. I split the job in two: senior managers kept only the client relationship, and one shared client care team took the admin for all of them. Before every hire I asked what it would take for a system to do this instead of a person. Client care became the route for junior hires to grow into managers.
AI that catches contract errors at signing
Prices and quantities that changed during negotiation weren't reaching the system of record, and errors surfaced only at billing. I put an AI agent into deal approval that read each signed contract and compared it with the system, so every difference was visible before final approval.

The methods are published in my newsletter as four frameworks: The Operations Stack (process, data and ownership come first, then automation, then reporting), The Improvement Stack (document how the work runs today and find the root cause before designing the fix), The Visible Value Loop (clients see their results in their own data, so renewals stop being negotiations), and The Compounding Deal Framework (each acquisition makes the next one easier).
